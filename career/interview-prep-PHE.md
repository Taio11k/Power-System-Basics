## 6. Vocabulary to own before the call (and the next round)

Say these naturally and you sound like someone who has been on a project.

**Documents and QA:** ITP (Inspection and Test Plan: what will be tested and witnessed) · ITR (Inspection and Test Record: the signed result sheet per item) · test pack (ITRs grouped per system/area) · MDR (Manufacturer's Data Report / handover dossier) · as-builts / redlines · RFI (request for information) · NCR (non-conformance report) · variation and EOT (extension of time) · lookahead · hold point and witness point · FAT / SAT (factory / site acceptance test) · punch list / defects · practical completion · PIMS (the completions database where ITRs are entered).

**Installation:** cable schedule · cable ladder/tray · gland and termination · loop check · megger (insulation resistance test) · continuity · earth continuity and earth-fault loop impedance · polarity · MCC (motor control centre) · VSD · switchroom · 3.3 kV / 11 kV / 415 V · arc-fault containment · LOTO / isolation and permit to work · HV switching.

**Standards (quote the number, not the content):** AS/NZS 3000:2018 Wiring Rules · AS/NZS 3008.1.1:2017 cable selection (current-carrying capacity, voltage drop, short-circuit temperature rise; Australian conditions 40 °C air / 25 °C soil) · AS/NZS 61439 series (LV switchgear and controlgear assemblies) · **AS/NZS 5368:2025 Electrical equipment in mines and quarries** (new in 2025; supersedes AS/NZS 3007:2013 and the AS/NZS 4871 series; knowing this exists will impress an engineering manager) · NSW Resources Regulator code of practice *Electrical engineering control plan* (EECP), May 2025, under the WHS (Mines and Petroleum Sites) legislation · AS 2067 substations and HV installations exceeding 1 kV (check the current edition on Standards Australia before quoting it).

Practice sentence: "On an MCC replacement I'd expect an ITP with hold points at pre-energisation IR and continuity tests, ITRs per cubicle and per cable, and all of it compiled into the MDR for Newmont at handover."

## 7. Questions to ask James (and the rapport question)

Ask two or three, not ten. Pick from:

**About the role (shows you read the ad):**
1. "Which project would this junior role be allocated to, and would it be Bathurst office based with site days at Cadia, or site based?"
2. "Who would the mentor be, a project engineer or the site supervisor? How many engineers are in the Bathurst team?"
3. "What does the ITR/MDR workload look like on a typical project, and which completions system do you use?" (You already know it is PIMS; asking lets him talk.)
4. "What does the rest of the process look like after this call, and who would I meet?"
5. "Is there a defined pathway from junior to project engineer at PHE, and do juniors rotate between the Mining and High Voltage divisions?"

**About him (this is what makes you memorable, done professionally):**
6. "You moved from the graduate talent coordinator role into talent acquisition at PHE; what do the juniors who do really well here have in common in their first six months?" (Uses his own career path, which is public on his profile, and makes him give you the answer key.)
7. "What do you enjoy about recruiting for a contractor like PHE compared with a big consultancy?"

**What not to do:** do not ask about his personal life, family, sport or where he lives. Nothing like that is public, you should not go looking, and in Australian professional culture it reads as odd on a first call. The memorable move is listening closely, using his first name, and **sending a short thank-you email the same day** that references one specific thing he said. Few candidates do that.

## 9. After the screen: the hiring-manager round

Expect a 30–60 min Teams or in-person interview with an engineering/project manager, possibly with a short technical chat, and possibly a Bathurst visit. Reviews suggest the whole process is quick ("about a week"). Prepare:

1. **Cable sizing walkthrough:** be able to size a 415 V motor feeder with AS/NZS 3008.1.1 logic (load current, derating for installation method and grouping, voltage drop limit, short-circuit withstand) in words. Pull course Step 2.3 (lines and cables) forward this month.
2. **Fault level and switchgear rating:** why a 1997 board fails arc-fault containment, what a fault-level study is for, how IEC 60909 gives the peak and breaking currents a board must be rated for. This is course Phase 4; read the Step 4.1 Learn section early.
3. **Safety:** isolation / LOTO, permit to work, why HV switching is a licensed activity, the EECP as the mine's electrical safety framework.
4. **Commissioning sequence:** mechanical completion → pre-commissioning (IR, continuity, loop checks, ITRs signed) → energisation → functional tests → handover with MDR.
5. **Bring one page:** a clean one-page summary of your MYMI control system (SLD or block diagram, I/O list, how you tested it). It is the closest thing you have to a site deliverable.

Add the answers you write for this round to `career/interview_answers.md` (the capstone phase expects that file anyway).
