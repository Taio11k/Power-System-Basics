# Glossary

Add every new term you meet, in your own words. Rewrite these starter entries once you understand them better.

## The grid

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bus (busbar)** | A node in the network where equipment connects: a conductor at one voltage that lines, transformers, generators and loads attach to. In a load flow, every bus has a voltage magnitude and angle. |
| **Single-line diagram (SLD)** | A drawing of a three-phase network using one line per circuit, with symbols for transformers, breakers, generators and loads. The standard way engineers show a network. |
| **Substation** | A site where voltage is transformed (stepped up or down) and circuits are switched and protected. |
| **Feeder** | A circuit leaving a substation that supplies a group of loads, usually radial in distribution networks. |
| **Transmission** | The high-voltage network carrying bulk power over long distances (in Australia's NEM: 500, 330, 275, 220 and 132 kV). |
| **Distribution** | The network that takes power from transmission substations to customers, at medium voltage (e.g. 11, 22, 33 kV) and low voltage. |
| **LV / MV / HV** | Low voltage is up to 1 kV AC (houses: 230/400 V). Medium voltage is commonly used for roughly 1–35 kV distribution. High voltage is above that, for transmission. |
| **230/400 V** | Low-voltage supply in Australia and IEC countries: 230 V phase-to-neutral, 400 V phase-to-phase (400 = √3 × 230). |
| **Transformer** | Equipment that changes AC voltage between two levels while (ideally) keeping power the same. |

## Power

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **kW (active power, P)** | The power that does useful work (heat, motion). |
| **kvar (reactive power, Q)** | Power that oscillates between source and load to build magnetic and electric fields. It does no net work but loads lines and affects voltage. |
| **kVA (apparent power, S)** | The combination of P and Q: S = √(P² + Q²). Equipment such as transformers and cables is rated in kVA because their current depends on S. |
| **Power factor** | P / S. 1.0 means all power is active; lower values mean more current for the same useful power. |

## Australian market and rules

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **NEM** | National Electricity Market: the interconnected grid and wholesale market covering QLD, NSW, ACT, VIC, SA and TAS. |
| **WEM** | Wholesale Electricity Market of Western Australia (the South West Interconnected System), separate from the NEM. |
| **AEMO** | Australian Energy Market Operator: runs the NEM and WEM, plans the system and assesses new connections. |
| **NER** | National Electricity Rules: the rules for the NEM. Chapter 5 covers network connection; Schedule 5.2 covers technical conditions for connecting generators. |
| **GPS** | Generator Performance Standards: the agreed technical performance a connected generating system must meet, set against the NER access standards. |
| **ISP** | Integrated System Plan: AEMO's long-term plan for the NEM's transmission and generation. |
| **System strength** | How well the grid holds a stable voltage waveform during and after disturbances. It is closely related to fault level and matters for inverter-based plants. |
