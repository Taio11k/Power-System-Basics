How the course works

Do the phases in order; each one depends on the last. Every step has the same parts.

Objective

What you will be able to do after the step.

Learn

Exactly which chapters, notes or docs to read. Reference codes like [GLOVER] are listed at the bottom.

Do

Numbered actions. Follow them literally.

Check

Worked answers to check yourself against, plus a self-check. Move on only when all of it is true.

Weekly rhythm (10–12 h)

2 × 2 h reading with pen and paper · 2 × 2–3 h coding and simulation · 1 × 1 h writing up your log and project README.

The validation rule

Never trust one tool. Check every number at least two ways: by hand, in Python, and in PowerFactory. Senior engineers value this habit above all.

When stuck for 45 minutes

Write down what you expected versus what you got, re-read the step's traps, then ask with that note.

Phase 1

AC and three-phase fundamentals

Weeks 2–4about 32 h

Solve any balanced AC circuit with phasors, compute P, Q, S and power factor, and convert line and phase quantities, by hand and in Python.

Main reading: [GLOVER] Ch. 2 · [VONMEIER] Ch. 3–4 · [MIT] Ch. 1–3

1.1Phasors and impedance

10 h
Learn
[VONMEIER] Ch. 3 (first part) · [GLOVER] Ch. 2: Phasors, Network Equations. Redo every example by hand.
Do
230 V, 50 Hz source with R = 10 Ω in series with L = 31.83 mH. Compute XL, Z and I in Python with complex numbers, then plot v(t) and i(t). Repeat with a capacitor (XC = 10 Ω).
Check
X_L ≈ 10.0 ΩZ ≈ 14.14∠45° ΩI ≈ 16.26 A ∠−45°

Trap: peak vs RMS (power engineers use RMS); cmath works in radians.

1.2Complex power, P, Q and power factor

10 h
Learn
[VONMEIER] Ch. 3 (rest) · [GLOVER] Ch. 2: Instantaneous Power, Complex Power.
Key idea
S = V · I* = P + jQ pf = cos φ = P / |S|
Do
  1. Write complex_power(V, I) and apply it to the Step 1.1 circuit.
  2. Size the capacitor that corrects a 100 kW load from pf 0.8 to 0.95.
Check
P ≈ 2645 WQ ≈ 2645 var|S| ≈ 3740 VAQ_C ≈ 42.1 kvar

Trap: using V·I instead of V·I*, which flips the sign of Q.

1.3Balanced three-phase systems

10 h
Learn
[VONMEIER] Ch. 4 · [GLOVER] Ch. 2: Balanced Three-Phase Circuits and Power · [MIT] Ch. 3.
Key idea
V_LL = √3 · V_LN S_3φ = √3 · V_LL · I_L Z_Y = Z_Δ / 3
Do
Plot the three phase voltages of a 230/400 V system and show their sum is zero. Solve a Y and a Δ load per phase. Start pslib/basics.py with reusable functions.
Check
400 V → 230.9 V L-N100 kVA at 400 V → 144.3 A

Trap: forgetting √3; mixing line and phase quantities.

✓Phase 1 checkpoint

2 h

Without notes: RMS of a 325 V peak sine · line current, Q and S of a 20 kW, pf 0.85 motor at 400 V · why a capacitor bank raises feeder-end voltage · why three-phase is used for transmission.

2.2The per-unit system

8 h

[GLOVER] Ch. 3: The Per-Unit System (redo every example) · [SAADAT] or [NPTEL] per-unit lectures.
Key idea
Z_base = U²/S I_base = S/(√3·U) Z_new = Z_old·(U_old/U_new)²·(S_new/S_old)

2.3Lines and cables

Phase 3 · Project P1

Power flow (load flow)

Weeks 8–10about 36 hTest system: WSCC 9-bus / MATPOWER case9

Understand a load flow solver from the inside by writing one, run professional load flow and N-1 studies, and publish P1.

Main reading: [GLOVER] Ch. 6 · [VONMEIER] Ch. 12–13 · [SAADAT] power flow chapter · [NPTEL] weeks 5–6 · [MIT] Ch. 5

3.1Bus types and the admittance matrix Ybus

8 h
Key idea
Slack bus: |V| and θ known. PV bus: P and |V| known. PQ bus: P and Q known. Si = Vi · (Σ Yij Vj)* is nonlinear, so it must be solved iteratively.
Do
Build a 3-bus Ybus by hand, then write build_ybus() and build Ybus for MATPOWER case9.

3.2Write your own Newton-Raphson solver

10 h
Do
  1. Flat start, then loop: compute P and Q, find the mismatch (ΔP for non-slack buses, ΔQ for PQ buses), stop below 1e-8 p.u.
  2. Build the Jacobian (numerically first, then analytically) and solve with numpy.linalg.solve.
  3. Validate on case9 against MATPOWER runpf('case9') and pandapower pn.case9(); add a test file.
Done when
All three agree to about 4 decimals in |V| and 0.01° in θ, converging in 3–6 iterations.

Trap: MW vs p.u. (divide by 100 MVA), missing line charging, 1-based vs 0-based bus numbers.

3.3Operating limits and controls

4 h
Key idea
Voltage limits come from the grid code or planning criteria. [EN50160] sets ±10 % for 95 % of 10-minute averages on public LV/MV supply. Thermal loading must stay ≤ 100 %. Generators have Q limits. Controls: AVR setpoints, OLTC taps, shunt capacitors and reactors.
Do
Scale case9 loads up in 10 % steps until a limit fails, then fix it with one measure and show before and after.

3.4The 9-bus system in PowerFactory

6 h
Do
Use the Examples window version if there is one, but verify its data. Otherwise build it from [ANDERSON]: generators at 16.5, 18 and 13.8 kV, step-up transformers to 230 kV, 6 lines, 3 loads, 100 MVA base. Compare with pandapower and make the single-line diagram presentable.

Note: pandapower's case9 comes from MATPOWER, and its bus numbering can differ from the Anderson & Fouad drawing. Match elements by their data.

3.5N-1 contingency analysis

4 h
Learn
[PP-N1] and the contingency_analysis.ipynb tutorial · PowerFactory manual: Contingency Analysis.
Do
Run run_contingency(net, {"line": {"index": ...}}) in pandapower and the contingency command in PowerFactory. Tabulate outage → worst loading → min/max voltage → violation, and mitigate the worst case.

Trap: outaging a generator step-up transformer islands the generator. Handle it explicitly.

Phase 4 · Project P2

Faults and IEC 60909 short circuit

Weeks 11–13about 34 hTest system: CIGRE MV benchmark (15 buses, 20 kV)

Calculate symmetrical and unsymmetrical fault currents by hand, run IEC 60909 studies in two tools, and check equipment ratings.

Main reading: [GLOVER] Ch. 8–10 · [MIT] Ch. 4 · [NPTEL] weeks 9–11 · [PP-SC] · [IEC60909]

4.1Symmetrical (three-phase) faults

8 h
Key idea
Ik = Uprefault / ZTh. IEC 60909 defines Ik″ (initial symmetrical current), ip (first peak including DC offset, for making capacity and mechanical forces) and Ith (thermal equivalent). Fault level Sk″ = √3 · Un · Ik″.
Do
Fault at the LV side of the 630 kVA, 4 % transformer fed from a 250 MVA grid, compared with the 22.7 kA infinite-grid answer. Redo one Zbus fault example from [GLOVER] Ch. 8.

4.2Symmetrical components

8 h
Key idea
a = 1∠120° [Va Vb Vc]ᵀ = A · [V0 V1 V2]ᵀ, A = [[1,1,1],[1,a²,a],[1,a,a²]]
Zero-sequence paths depend on earthing and vector group: a Dyn transformer blocks zero-sequence current between its sides.
Do
Write abc_to_012 and 012_to_abc, reproduce textbook examples, and hand-draw zero-sequence networks for YNd and Dyn transformers.

4.3Unsymmetrical faults

6 h
Key idea
SLG: Ia = 3·Vf / (Z1+Z2+Z0) LL: Ib = −j·√3·Vf / (Z1+Z2)
Do
Code SLG, LL and DLG in pslib/faults.py. Show when an SLG fault exceeds a three-phase fault (Z0 < Z1).

4.4IEC 60909 in practice

8 h
Key idea
Equivalent voltage source c·Un/√3 at the fault; loads and line capacitance neglected. cmax for equipment rating, cmin for protection sensitivity. Look up the c values in IEC 60909 Table 1 and cite them; do not guess. The 2016 edition covers full-converter units such as PV and batteries.
Do
  1. pandapower: sc.calc_sc(net, case="max", fault="3ph") on create_cigre_network_mv(), then the min case and 1-phase faults (add any missing zero-sequence data and record your assumptions).
  2. Export with pp.to_excel() and build the same network in PowerFactory. Run IEC 60909 max/min, 3-phase and 1-phase.
  3. Compare Ik″ and ip per bus, check against assumed switchgear ratings, and repeat with with_der="pv_wind".

Trap: comparing max in one tool with min in the other; motor or generator contributions on in only one tool.

Portfolio project P2

IEC 60909 short-circuit study of the CIGRE MV network

Bus-by-bus table of Ik″(max), ip and Ik″(min), a colour-coded fault-level SLD, an equipment adequacy check, the DER effect, pandapower vs PowerFactory validation, and one hand-calculated bus.

5.1Protection philosophy and hardware

8 h
Key idea
Selectivity, sensitivity, speed, reliability. Primary vs backup protection and zones. CT ratios and saturation. Device types: fuse, breaker + relay, recloser; ANSI 50/51, 50N/51N, 87, 21.

5.2IDMT curves and your own TCC tool

8 h

Curvekα
Standard inverse0.140.02
Very inverse13.51
Extremely inverse802
Long-time inverse1201
Confirm the constants against the standard, [GERS] or a relay manual, and cite the source.
Do
Write idmt_time(), a definite-time stage, and a log-log plot_tcc().
Check

5.3Coordinate a radial feeder

10 h
Do
  1. Choose a radial CIGRE MV feeder. Place R3 near the end, R2 mid-feeder, R1 at the head.
  2. Collect max load current, max fault current and min end-of-zone fault current for each relay (from P1 and P2).
  3. Pickup above max load (factor from [GERS], cited) and below the min fault current, including the backup zone. Choose CT ratios.
  4. Set R3 at the lowest TMS. Grade R2 over R3 at the max current both see, using a margin from [GERS]; then grade R1 over R2.
  5. Plot all curves with fault-current lines and list the first relay to trip for each bus.
  6. Verify in PowerFactory protection if licensed; otherwise use pandapower's protection/oc_relay.ipynb for your installed version.

Trap: grading at the wrong current; min fault current below pickup; mixing primary and secondary amps.

Phase 6 · Project P4

Distribution networks and solar integration

Weeks 17–19about 34 h

Run time-series studies, calculate PV hosting capacity, test voltage-control mitigations, and publish P4.

Main reading: [VONMEIER] Ch. 5, 7, 14 · [GLOVER] Ch. 15 · [BOLLEN] Ch. 3 · [KERSTING] · [EN50160]

6.1Voltage rise from PV

8 h
Key idea
ΔU ≈ (P·R + Q·X)/U. PV raises voltage, most where R/X is high (LV cables). Absorbing reactive power reduces the rise.
Do
On create_cigre_network_lv(), add PV at a feeder end and increase P until the voltage limit, then repeat with Q = −0.3·P. Plot U vs P.

6.2Time-series simulation

8 h
Learn
pandapower time_series.ipynb and time_series_multiple_loads_generators.ipynb · PowerFactory manual: Quasi-Dynamic Simulation.
Do
Build 96-step daily load and PV profiles (pandapower's cigre_timeseries_15min.json or labelled synthetic ones). Use DFData, ConstControl, OutputWriter and run_timeseries. Plot feeder-end voltage at 0, 50 and 100 % PV, then repeat in PowerFactory.

6.3Hosting capacity

8 h
Learn
[BOLLEN] Ch. 3: performance index → limit → hosting capacity · pandapower hosting_capacity.ipynb.
Do
Define and cite the criteria first. Deterministic: increase PV per bus until the first limit fails (bar chart). Stochastic: random placement, about 500 runs, report the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles.

6 h

Phase 7 · Project P5

Stability and an EMT introduction

Weeks 20–22about 34 h

Find a critical clearing time analytically, numerically and in PowerFactory RMS simulation (automated with Python), and know when EMT is required.

Main reading: [GLOVER] Ch. 12 · [SAADAT] stability chapter · [P-STAB2021] · [KUNDUR] or [MACHOWSKI]

7.1Stability classification

3 h
Learn
[P-STAB2021]: rotor angle, voltage, frequency, and the 2021 additions, converter-driven and resonance stability. Redraw the tree.

7.2Swing equation and equal-area criterion

9 h
Key idea
(2H/ω_s)·d²δ/dt² = P_m − P_max·sin δ
cos δ_cr = (π − 2δ_0)·sin δ_0 − cos δ_0 t_cr = √(4H(δ_cr − δ_0) / (ω_s·P_m))
Do
H = 5 s, 50 Hz, Pm = 0.9, Pmax = 1.8 p.u. Integrate with solve_ivp for tc = 0.20 s and 0.30 s, then find tcr by bisection.
Check
δ_0 = 30°δ_cr ≈ 79.6°t_cr ≈ 0.247 s

7.3PowerFactory RMS simulation, automated

10 h
Do
  1. Add dynamic data from [ANDERSON] to your 9-bus model.
  2. Apply a 3-phase line fault at 0.1 s and clear it by tripping the line. Plot relative rotor angles.
  3. With import powerfactory, loop: set clearing time → ComInc + ComSim → read angles → stable? → bisect. Save CSV and plots.
  4. Repeat for 3 fault locations.

Trap: absolute rotor angles drift, so use relative ones; state the load model. If RMS is not licensed, do the multi-machine study in Python or MATLAB and say so.

7.4EMT with PSCAD

8 h
Learn
PSCAD tutorial projects in order. [AEMO] uses PSS®E for RMS and PSCAD for EMT connection studies.
Do
20 kV source → breaker → 3-phase fault; apply it at voltage zero and at voltage peak and observe the DC offset. Compare the worst peak with IEC 60909: κ = 1.02 + 0.98·e^(−3R/X), i_p = κ·√2·I_k″.

Interview preparation

Answer each one aloud in under 2 minutes before you apply.

  1. What is reactive power and why does it matter for voltage?
  2. Explain the per-unit system and why we use it.
  3. What are PQ, PV and slack buses?
  4. How does Newton-Raphson load flow work, and when does it fail to converge?
  5. What does N-1 mean? Give a violation and a fix.
  6. Difference between Ik″, ip and Ith, and which rating each checks?
  7. Why does IEC 60909 use a voltage factor c, and why max and min cases?
  8. Why can an SLG fault exceed a three-phase fault?
  9. What does Dyn11 mean, and why does it matter for earth faults?
  10. How do you grade two IDMT relays, and what is the margin made of?
  11. What is protection blinding?
  12. Why does PV raise feeder voltage, and how does Q(U) help?
  13. Define hosting capacity.
  14. What is critical clearing time? Explain the equal-area criterion.
  15. When do you need EMT instead of RMS simulation?
  16. What is the short-circuit ratio and why do inverter-based plants care?
  17. Walk me through your capstone: the biggest constraint and the mitigation.
  18. Tell me about a time two tools disagreed. How did you find out why?
